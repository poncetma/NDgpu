# Step 10a: dynamic energy-mode acceleration

**Decision:** split by backend. On CPU, accept three forward scatter sweeps,
depth-one Anderson, and `inner_rtol=0.1`. On the Tesla T4, reject that same
tolerance-based Anderson path. Accept the four-sweep reduction-free
fixed-polynomial path as the T4 opt-in: three interleaved samples give 1.101x
over plain group PCG and 1.249x over tolerance-based Anderson.

## Why the spatial CMFD correction did not help

The Step 09a HP-MR outer count stayed at 25 as the spatial mesh was refined.
That ruled out a mesh-dependent spatial diffusion mode. Several coarse spaces
were tested against the remaining group/fission coupling:

| candidate, refinement 1 | outer iterations | conclusion |
|---|---:|---|
| three forward group sweeps | 26 | baseline |
| adjoint-weighted rank one | 23 | useful, but an adjoint eigen solve is expensive |
| 11 group-amplitude modes | 23 | no gain beyond rank one; 11 setup sweeps |
| five material/source modes | 22 | setup cost erases the extra iteration |
| finer source-region modes | 29--32 | static regional shapes make the preconditioner worse |
| reverse/alternating group order | 28 to failure | wrong propagation ordering for this library |
| sweep relaxation, 0.7--1.5 | 24--31 | fewer outer steps do not repay extra PCG work |
| depth-two Anderson | 22 | same result as depth one, with more storage/reductions |

Static modes do not follow the error generated by a changed transient
operator closely enough. The accepted method instead estimates that direction
from the current preconditioner application.

## Algorithm

Let `G(x)` be one forward energy-group Gauss--Seidel subsweep for the fixed
right-hand side supplied to the preconditioner. After two subsweeps, define

```text
f_k       = G(x_k) - x_k
delta_f   = f_k - f_(k-1)
delta_g   = G(x_k) - G(x_(k-1))
gamma     = dot(delta_f, f_k) / dot(delta_f, delta_f)
x_(k+1)   = G(x_k) - gamma * delta_g
```

This is depth-one Walker--Ni Anderson acceleration. It is applied between the
three group subsweeps inside each flexible-GMRES preconditioner call. FGMRES
already permits a nonlinear/variable right preconditioner, so its outer
mathematics and true-residual stopping test are unchanged.

The implementation owns six persistent full-state work arrays. It adds two
device reductions per accelerated subsweep, but the coefficients remain
zero-dimensional backend arrays: there is no Python-float conversion and no
GPU-to-host synchronization. It adds no fine multigroup operator application,
no adjoint solve, and no operator-dependent setup.

## Simplified energy-chain gate

An 11-group, one-cell bidirectional scatter chain isolates energy propagation
from spatial diffusion. With three subsweeps, depth-one Anderson reduces the
preconditioner defect from `1.495e-2` to `7.036e-3`, a factor of 2.12. It also
preserves homogeneity and the converged FGMRES solution.

## Two-dimensional HP-MR gate

The production-representative gate is the 11-group, 20 ms bulk-absorption
insertion used in Step 09a. Both methods use three forward group sweeps,
within-group PCG tolerance `1e-3`, degree-one polynomial PCG preconditioning,
FGMRES restart 30, and relative tolerance `1e-9`. Timings are medians of three
interleaved CPU samples.

| refine | active cells | method | outer | inner PCG | median solve (s) | speedup |
|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 330 | baseline | 26 | 1,304 | 0.1875 | -- |
| 1 | 330 | energy Anderson | 22 | 1,083 | 0.1630 | 1.15x |
| 2 | 1,320 | baseline | 25 | 2,112 | 0.3939 | -- |
| 2 | 1,320 | energy Anderson | 20 | 1,709 | 0.3286 | 1.20x |
| 3 | 2,970 | baseline | 25 | 2,995 | 0.8507 | -- |
| 3 | 2,970 | energy Anderson | 20 | 2,381 | 0.6967 | 1.22x |

The gain grows slightly with refinement: outer work falls by 15--20%, inner
PCG work by 17--21%, and CPU time by 13--18%. Four or six accelerated sweeps
reduce the outer count further (19 and 16 at refinement 1), but extra group
solves make three sweeps the consistent timing choice at refinements 2 and 3.
Putting lagged fission inside the sweep similarly lowers the outer count but
raises total inner work and wall time, so it remains disabled.

### Inexact inner-solve gate

Flexible GMRES permits deliberately inexact group solves and always checks the
true multigroup residual. Loosening group PCG from `1e-3` to `0.1` is the
larger practical win. Values `0.15--0.2` begin increasing outer work, while
`0.3` and above deteriorate sharply. With `0.1`, three sweeps, and depth one:

| refine | original baseline outer / inner | accepted outer / inner | accepted median (s) | speedup vs original |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 26 / 1,304 | 22 / 440 | 0.1179 | 1.59x |
| 2 | 25 / 2,112 | 22 / 649 | 0.2615 | 1.51x |
| 3 | 25 / 2,995 | 24 / 949 | 0.5327 | 1.60x |

These medians use three interleaved samples. The combined configuration cuts
inner PCG work by 66--69% and CPU time by 34--37%. The outer residuals remain
below `1e-9`, and flux/power differences against fixed point are unchanged.
Degree-one polynomial PCG preconditioning remains optimal: degree zero raises
outer/inner work, while degrees two and three spend more stencil work than
their small iteration reduction repays.

Per-group tolerances were also measured. Tightening groups 0--1 and loosening
groups 8--10 saves one outer iteration at refinement 2, but increases outer
and inner work at refinements 1 and 3. The uniform `0.1` policy is therefore
retained rather than embedding an HP-MR-library-specific tuning vector.

### Reduction-free GPU candidate

A second implementation replaces tolerance-based group PCG with a fixed
polynomial/Richardson correction. Four sweeps with one degree-one correction
and depth-one Anderson are slower on CPU, but contain no inner convergence
checks or CG dot products. This is retained only for direct GPU A/B testing.
Fixed one-step PCG was also tested and rejected in favor of the polynomial
variant: it requires an additional stencil application and device reductions.

## Submitted Tesla T4 gate

The submitted artifact is
`notebooks/notebook-results-json/hpmr3d_adaptive_bdf_gpu.json`: Tesla T4,
CuPy 14.0.1, Python 3.12.13, refinement 2 x 10 axial layers, 13,200 active
cells, 145,200 flux unknowns, 11 groups, and FGMRES restart 10.

Two submitted short-gate samples show the same ordering but considerable
fixed-polynomial timing variation:

| short one-step path | first (s) | second (s) | outer | reported inner work |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| plain tolerance PCG, three sweeps | 2.139 | 2.069 | 63 | 1,944 PCG |
| tolerance PCG + depth-one Anderson | 3.265 | 2.432 | 71 | 2,199 PCG |
| fixed polynomial + depth-one Anderson, four sweeps | 1.457 | 2.013 | 83 | 3,652 relaxations |

Tolerance-based Anderson loses in both runs (0.655x and 0.851x versus plain).
The fixed polynomial wins by 1.468x in the first run but only 1.028x in the
second. That spread motivated a repeated, interleaved gate.

The final three-sample result is:

| short path | samples (s) | median (s) | speedup vs plain |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| plain tolerance PCG | 2.310, 2.256, 2.327 | 2.310 | baseline |
| tolerance PCG + Anderson | 2.620, 2.516, 2.906 | 2.620 | 0.882x |
| fixed polynomial + Anderson | 2.275, 1.912, 2.098 | 2.098 | **1.101x** |

The fixed polynomial is also 1.249x faster than tolerance-based Anderson.
This closes the T4 short-gate decision: fixed polynomial is accepted opt-in;
tolerance-based Anderson is rejected on GPU.

The CPU and GPU rankings differ because the T4 pays heavily for every PCG
convergence reduction and small-kernel synchronization. Tolerance-based
Anderson also increases outer work in this 3-D restart-10 case, so its CPU gain
cannot be assumed portable. The fixed-polynomial path deliberately performs
more cheap stencil/AXPY work but removes the inner convergence checks and CG
dot products; its `inner` count is therefore not directly comparable with PCG
iterations.

The first main adaptive legs still used the now-rejected
tolerance-based Anderson configuration. They pass all accuracy gates, but are
not the final performance measurement: adaptive BDF5 took 42.164 s versus
39.450 s for adaptive BE, or 0.936x. BDF used 22/9 accepted/rejected attempts
against 24/7 and again remained mostly order one (15 q1, 7 q2).

### Full fixed-polynomial run

The replacement artifact uses the fixed-polynomial configuration in all main
legs and passes the same reference gates:

| method | accepted / rejected | march (s) | outer incl. rejects | fixed relaxations | max power error | flux-shape error |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| fine fixed BE | 32 / 0 | 32.958 | 2,077 | 91,388 | reference | reference |
| adaptive BE | 24 / 7 | 34.029 | 2,062 | 90,728 | 3.007e-4 | 1.379e-6 |
| adaptive BDF5 | 22 / 9 | 35.977 | 2,260 | 99,440 | 2.604e-4 | 2.744e-7 |

Relative to the prior Anderson run, the fixed-polynomial configuration is
1.115x faster for fine BE, 1.159x for adaptive BE, and 1.172x for adaptive
BDF5. It uses roughly twice as many outer iterations, but adaptive-BE
neutronics cost per outer falls from 32.8 ms to 14.9 ms; the BDF value falls
from 33.3 ms to 14.5 ms. This confirms the intended GPU throughput mechanism.

Adaptive BDF5 remains slower than matched adaptive BE at 0.946x. Its two extra
rejected attempts raise total outer work 9.6%; the mild motion still provides
only seven order-two steps and no sustained high-order advantage.

The repeated-gate session reproduces the same numerical history and work
counts. Its fine-BE/adaptive-BE/adaptive-BDF5 times are 36.578/36.056/39.971 s,
and matched BDF performance is 0.902x. Across both fixed-polynomial sessions,
BDF is therefore 5.7--10.9% slower than adaptive BE. The stable cause is work,
not timing: 2,260 versus 2,062 outer iterations after BDF's two additional
rejected attempts.

All accelerated true residuals are below `7e-10`. Against the independent
fixed-point step, the relative flux differences are `1.41e-6`, `4.97e-7`, and
`2.35e-7` for refinements 1--3; the corresponding absolute power differences
are `1.96e-6`, `6.97e-7`, and `3.29e-7`. These are the same fixed-point stopping
differences seen without acceleration.

## Interface and GPU gate

The production monolithic solver accepts:

```python
multigroup_kwargs={
    "scatter_sweeps": 3,
    "energy_anderson": 1,
    "inner_rtol": 0.1,
}
```

The CPU default remains conservative; use the configuration above explicitly.
The refreshed `colab_hpmr3d_adaptive_bdf_gpu.ipynb` now uses this GPU path for
its main legs:

```python
multigroup_kwargs={
    "scatter_sweeps": 4,
    "energy_anderson": 1,
    "inner_fixed_relaxations": 1,
}
```

It retains both short A/B gates, running three interleaved repetitions and
exporting samples plus medians so future hardware changes remain measurable.
